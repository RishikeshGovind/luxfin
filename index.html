Research Context
Luxembourg's general government has historically maintained surpluses (pre-COVID: ~2.5–3% of GDP), reflecting strong financial centre tax revenues (financial services, holding companies) and conservative fiscal management. The COVID shock (2020) produced a rare near-zero balance (0.3% surplus), swiftly reversed. The communal layer (~11% of total expenditure) is relevant for subnational fiscal analysis: communes depend heavily on transfers from the central government and on local business taxes, creating fiscal linkages that can be studied using STATEC LAU-level data.

Methodology
Each component is normalised to [0,1] using domain-specific thresholds, then aggregated with fixed weights: Bond concentration (30%), Public credit share (30%), Fiscal balance (25%), Sector size (15%). A score below 0.25 is classified as Low stress.

Scope limitation: This is a deterministic descriptive index with static weights and no prediction horizon. Converting it to a forward-looking early-warning system (as proposed in the PhD research design) requires: labelled stress events, a forecasting model, out-of-sample validation, calibration, lead-time tests, and false-alarm rate analysis.

Research Note — Bank-Sovereign Nexus in Luxembourg
Luxembourg presents a structurally atypical case: despite a large banking sector (~12× GDP), domestic sovereign risk is minimal (Maastricht debt ~25% GDP, AAA/Aaa rating, persistent surpluses). The dominant transmission channel is therefore outward — Luxembourg banks hold EA sovereign bonds (primarily French, Belgian, Italian, Spanish), and are exposed to contagion through the European sovereign risk channel. The ECB's PSPP/PEPP programmes significantly increased government bond holdings post-2015, a pattern visible in the bond concentration series. The composite stress score remaining below 0.25 throughout the sample period reflects Luxembourg's structural fiscal strength, though the large banking sector creates systemic risk at the EA level.
